# Epinets primer for EpinetLens

Reference notes on the framework in Moldoveanu & Baum, *Epinets: The Epistemic Structure and Dynamics of Social Networks* (2014). Written as a paraphrase of the book's definitions, for use as background by the analyst. Use these terms in their defined senses, and explain a term in plain words the first time it appears.

## 1. The basic object

An **epinet** (epistemic network) is a map with two kinds of nodes, **agents** and **propositions**, joined by epistemic links: who knows or believes which proposition, and who knows or believes what about others' knowledge and beliefs. Ordinary network analysis asks who is tied to whom. An epinet asks what each agent knows, what each thinks the others know, and what each thinks the others think they know. The book calls this layer the "epistemic glue" that makes social ties matter.

A **proposition** is a statement with definite truth conditions. Analysis starts by stating the propositions at stake in a text as explicit sentences.

## 2. An agent's relation to a proposition

- **Knowledge:** the agent believes it and it is true.
- **Belief:** the agent credits it, short of knowledge.
- **Awareness:** the agent knows it and knows that they know it.
- **Unawareness:** the agent knows it but not readily; they would recognize it if shown.
- **Ignorance:** the agent knows they do not know whether it is true.
- **Oblivion:** the agent does not know it and does not know that they do not know it.

Ignorance and oblivion behave differently. An ignorant agent can go looking; an oblivious agent cannot, and can be acted on without noticing.

## 3. Levels of interactive knowledge

- **Level 1:** what A knows or believes.
- **Level 2:** what A thinks B knows or believes ("I think you think").
- **Level 3:** what A thinks B thinks A knows ("I think you think I think").
- **Mutual knowledge:** each knows that the other knows.
- **Almost-common knowledge:** the chain holds up to some finite level.
- **Common knowledge:** the chain holds without limit.

Much of what a text does happens at levels 2 and 3. A public statement often changes nobody's level 1 beliefs; it changes what everyone knows that everyone else now knows.

## 4. Structure: who can act together

- **Cohesion** is coherence among agents' interactive epistemic states, not density of ties.
- **Co-mobilization** (acting only if enough others will) requires mutual knowledge of each other's rule and threshold for acting.
- **Coordination** is more demanding and can require close to common knowledge.
- **Coordinata:** the propositions that must be almost-common knowledge for a group to coordinate.
- **Mobilizer:** an agent who knows what most others know.
- **Coordinator:** an agent whom most others know to know what everyone knows or believes.
- **Brokers** span cohesive subgroups and carry more than information: they can broker trust, awareness and higher-level knowledge.
- **Epistemic centrality:** for an agent, how widely their epistemic states are known; for a proposition, how widely it is known and known to be known.

## 5. Status in epistemic terms

- **Knownness:** the share of agents who know of an agent.
- **Fame / infamy:** the share who know the agent has some positive / negative property.
- **Glory / notoriety:** the share who know who knows that positive / negative property.
- **Renown:** the share who know all those who know of the agent.
- **Reach:** the share whose epistemic states change on learning the agent's state.
- **Clout:** the same, counted when the agent's state differs from their own.

## 6. Trust and authentication

Trust is an epistemic relation that safeguards the registration and passage of relevant information. It has two parts:

- **Trust in competence:** A knows that if a relevant proposition were true, B would know it.
- **Trust in integrity:** A knows that if B knew it, B would say so to A.

Full trust combines both: B is taken to deliver the truth, the whole truth and nothing but the truth. Weaker forms exist, for example trusting B to say what they are aware of, or to say true things without saying everything. When trust is breached, the trustor must decide which part failed, and the two failures call for different repairs. Trust can also be broken without appearing to be broken.

Structures built from trust:

- **Trust neighborhood:** a subgroup linked by trust, without common knowledge of who belongs.
- **Trust conduit / corridor:** a one-way / two-way chain of trust links along which information travels credibly.
- **Security neighborhood, conduit, corridor:** the same structures where membership is common knowledge among members.

Common knowledge of membership is the basis of **authentication**: members can tell at once whether a message comes from inside. In a mere trust neighborhood, an outsider's contribution cannot be authenticated without checking. The book's distinction between fact and rumor follows: a **fact** has travelled along a trust conduit; a **rumor** has not.

**Covert** communication uses a shared private key (for example, a reference only two parties understand) so that a third party hears the signal but stays oblivious of its content, and may not know a code was used at all. Covert and selective communication can be more or less **auditable** and **deniable** after the fact.

## 7. Moves and strategies

A **move** is an action that changes the truth value of a relevant proposition, the interactive epistemic structure, or both. A **strategy** is a sequence of moves aimed at a particular effect.

Moves on the relational structure (what agents think other agents think):

- **Divisive moves** create asymmetries: they shift a group away from common knowledge, undercut an agent's reach or clout, or weaken trust and security structures. Selective disclosure and selective timing are typical.
- **Collusive moves** do the reverse: they build common knowledge of a shared core, extend reach and clout, and establish or repair trust structures.

Moves on the propositions themselves (the epistemic core):

- **Distortive moves:**
  - **Lie:** asserting what the speaker knows to be false. Its effect depends on the trust structure of the link, and a "white" lie that the hearer knows to be one can function as an encrypted signal aimed at a third party.
  - **Palter:** colouring the truth without stating a falsehood, so the hearer's resulting beliefs do not track what is true.
  - **Bullshit:** trying to shift the hearer's beliefs about something whose truth the speaker does not know.
- **Clarifying moves** push communication toward tracking all and only the truth:
  - **Substantive:** auditing and filtering messages without attacking the sender.
  - **Sanctioning:** publicly unmasking a distorter and so reducing others' trust in them.
  - **Interrogatory:** questioning to unpack a message.
  - **Declarative:** openly challenging it.

For any move, separate **intent** from **effect**. Both depend on what sender and receiver know about each other.

## 8. Resilience

- **Stability:** the epinet's states and structures hold when nothing in the world has changed, and its trust structures survive new information.
- **Robustness:** it withstands unintentional error and noise in transmission.
- **Immunity:** it withstands intentional distortion and attacks on its trust and security structures.

An epinet is fragile where a few trust brokers connect otherwise separate groups, or where a security structure depends on beliefs that can be put in doubt. Introducing uncertainty about whether trust was kept or broken is itself an attack.

## 10. Limits

These definitions describe structure. Applying them to a text is interpretation: say when a claim about what an agent knows or intends is an inference from the text and not something the text states.
